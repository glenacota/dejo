📚 German Noun & Plural Rules

☠️ No single rule applies to all nouns. Every German noun has a specific plural form that must be learned along with its gender and singular form.
  • Most feminine nouns take -en (or -n if ending in -e, -el, -er). Almost none take an umlaut. (e.g., die Frau → die Frauen).
  • Many masculine and neuter nouns take -e, frequently adding an umlaut to root vowels a, o, u (e.g., der Baum → die Bäume).
  • Nouns ending in -er, -el, -en usually take no extra ending, though masculine/neuter variants often add an umlaut (e.g., der Apfel → die Äpfel; das Zimmer → die Zimmer).
  • Words ending in vowels (a, o, u, i, y) and many foreign words take an -s with no umlaut (e.g., das Auto → die Autos).
  • A large group of short neuter nouns take -er and add an umlaut (e.g., das Kind → die Kinder; das Buch → die Bücher).
  • Nouns ending in -e, -ant, -ent, -ist, -or for people/occupations almost always add -n or -en (e.g., der Student → die Studenten).
